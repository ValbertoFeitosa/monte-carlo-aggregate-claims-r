Model:
Collective Risk Model with a Compound Poisson distribution

------------------------------------------------------------
MODEL PARAMETERS
------------------------------------------------------------

Claim Frequency:
N ~ Poisson(50)

Claim Severity:
X ~ Lognormal(mu = 3, sigma = 0.5)

Number of Monte Carlo Simulations:
10,000

Random Seed:
123

SIMULATED RESULTS
------------------------------------------------------------

Mean of Aggregate Loss:
1139.30

Variance of Aggregate Loss:
33261.80

Standard Deviation of Aggregate Loss:
182.38

------------------------------------------------------------
THEORETICAL RESULTS
------------------------------------------------------------

Expected Value of Claim Severity:
E[X] = 22.7599

Variance of Claim Severity:
Var(X) = 147.129

Expected Aggregate Loss:
E[S] = 1137.99

Variance of Aggregate Loss:
Var(S) = 33257.10

Standard Deviation of Aggregate Loss:
SD(S) = 182.37

------------------------------------------------------------
NORMAL APPROXIMATION
------------------------------------------------------------

S approximately follows:

Normal(
  mean = 1137.99,
  variance = 33257.10
)

Standard Deviation:
182.37

------------------------------------------------------------
TRANSLATED GAMMA APPROXIMATION
------------------------------------------------------------

Shape Parameter:
alpha = 94.4733

Rate Parameter:
beta = 0.0532982

Translation Parameter:
x0 = -634.548

Approximation:

S = x0 + Y

where:

Y ~ Gamma(
  shape = 94.4733,
  rate = 0.0532982
)

------------------------------------------------------------
CONCLUSION
------------------------------------------------------------

The Monte Carlo simulation produced results that were in
excellent agreement with the theoretical moments of the
aggregate loss distribution.

The Normal approximation provided satisfactory results due
to the relatively large Poisson parameter, which supports
the application of the Central Limit Theorem.

The Translated Gamma approximation also produced consistent
results, preserving the theoretical mean and variance of the
aggregate loss distribution.

Both approximations provided results close to those obtained
by Monte Carlo simulation for the model considered in this
project.
